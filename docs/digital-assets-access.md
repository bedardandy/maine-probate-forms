# Accessing a Deceased Person's Online Accounts (Maine)

> **Considerations, not legal advice.** This is a research aid for a personal
> representative, conservator, heir, or beneficiary trying to reach a deceased
> person's digital accounts. It is **not legal advice** and not a substitute for
> a licensed Maine attorney. Provider processes change frequently — every
> provider detail below is **as of 2026-05** and must be re-checked against the
> provider's current help page before relying on it.

When someone dies, two different questions get tangled together:

1. **Closing / memorializing** an account (stop billing, freeze or delete it), and
2. **Getting the contents** out (emails, messages, photos, documents, money).

Closing is usually straightforward with a death certificate and proof you are
next of kin or the estate's representative. **Getting contents** is the hard
part, because federal privacy law and provider terms restrict disclosure of the
*content* of electronic communications. This document maps the Maine statute that
gives a fiduciary authority, the federal law that gates content, the probate
forms that produce your proof of authority, and each major provider's process.

---

## 1. The Maine statutory hook — 18-C Article 10 (RUFADAA)

Maine's **Revised Uniform Fiduciary Access to Digital Assets Act** is codified at
**18-C M.R.S. §§ 10-101 to 10-118** (Article 10 of the Probate Code). It is the
state-law authority a fiduciary uses to ask a custodian (Google, Apple, etc.) to
disclose a decedent's digital assets.

Key sections to consider:

- **[§10-104](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/18-C/title18-Csec10-104.html)
  — User direction for disclosure.** A user can use an **online tool** (e.g.
  Google Inactive Account Manager, Facebook Legacy Contact, Apple Legacy Contact)
  to say what happens to their digital assets. That online-tool direction
  **overrides** a contrary will or terms of service. This is the single most
  important planning fact: a tool set up before death is the cleanest path.
- **[§10-105](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/18-C/title18-Csec10-105.html)
  — Terms of service.** Absent a user direction, the provider's TOS controls;
  Article 10 does not force a custodian to violate other law.
- **[§10-106](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/18-C/title18-Csec10-106.html)
  — Procedure for disclosing digital assets.** Lets a custodian require a written
  request, a death certificate, and a certified copy of the letters of
  appointment, and to demand a court order in defined circumstances.
- **[§10-107](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/18-C/title18-Csec10-107.html)
  — Content of electronic communications of a deceased user.** Disclosure of
  **content** (the actual text of emails/messages) requires more: the user's
  consent (often via the online tool or will) **and** typically a court order
  reflecting the findings that make disclosure lawful under the federal Stored
  Communications Act.
- **[§10-108](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/18-C/title18-Csec10-108.html)
  — Other digital assets of a deceased user.** A **catalogue** of communications
  (who/when, not content) and non-content digital assets is disclosed on a lower
  showing than content.
- **[§10-114](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/18-C/title18-Csec10-114.html)
  — Conservator access.** A conservator of a living protected person can get
  access (often with a court order) — relevant to the guardianship/conservatorship
  forms, not just death.
- **[§10-115](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/18-C/title18-Csec10-115.html)
  — Fiduciary duty and authority.** A fiduciary's authority over digital assets is
  subject to the same fiduciary duties (care, loyalty) as other property, and a
  fiduciary with authority over the decedent's property is an "authorized user"
  for computer-fraud/unauthorized-access purposes.

### Why content is gated — the federal Stored Communications Act

The **Stored Communications Act (18 U.S.C. §2701 et seq.)** bars a provider from
disclosing the *content* of communications except with lawful consent. RUFADAA
(§10-107) is drafted to supply that consent pathway, which is why providers ask
for a court order that *recites the SCA-relevant findings*: that the decedent was
the account user, that you are the lawful representative/heir, and that your
request constitutes the user's lawful consent to disclosure. **Non-content** items
(account closure, a catalogue, files in a drive, money) generally do not need that
order.

## 2. Which Maine probate forms produce your proof of authority

Custodians under §10-106 typically want a **certified copy of letters** plus a
**death certificate**. In this repo's forms:

| You need | Form(s) | What it produces |
|---|---|---|
| Appointment as personal representative (intestate) | **DE-101** → letters | Authority to act for the estate |
| Appointment as PR (testate / with a will) | **DE-201** → letters | Authority + will-based directions |
| Urgent interim authority | **DE-301** (special administrator) | Limited, immediate authority |
| Small estate, no full administration | **AF-102** (small-estate affidavit, [§3-1201](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/18-C/title18-Csec3-1201.html)) | Successor's authority to collect personal property ≤ $40,000 |
| Conservatorship over a living person's assets | **PP-107 / PP-401** (+ letters) | Conservator authority ([§10-114](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/18-C/title18-Csec10-114.html)) |
| Proof the estate is closed / discharge | **DE-603 / DE-605** | Wind-down documentation |

A **death certificate** is obtained from Maine vital records (Title 22), not from
this court system. Order several certified copies — every provider wants one.

> **Note on the small-estate affidavit (AF-102):** it gives you authority to
> *collect personal property*, which many custodians accept for **closure** and
> non-content assets. It is **not** "letters" and may be insufficient where a
> provider demands letters or a court order for **content**.

## 3. Provider-by-provider request mechanisms (as of 2026-05)

For every provider: gather a **government photo ID** for yourself, a **certified
death certificate**, and your **proof of authority** (letters or affidavit)
before you start. "Content" below means actual emails/messages/files.

### Google (Gmail, Drive, Photos, YouTube, Google Pay)
- **Plan-ahead tool:** [Inactive Account Manager](https://myaccount.google.com/inactive)
  — overrides other directions under §10-104 if the decedent set it up.
- **After death:** [Submit a request regarding a deceased user's account](https://support.google.com/accounts/troubleshooter/6357590)
  — choose close the account, request funds, or request data.
- **Requires:** your ID + certified death certificate; for funds/data, **court-certified
  letters** (Google accepts letters testamentary **or** letters of administration).
- **Content:** Google generally requires a **U.S. court order** before releasing
  the *contents* of a deceased person's account; closure and some data requests do not.

### Apple (Apple Account / iCloud, photos, files, purchases)
- **Plan-ahead tool:** [Legacy Contact](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102631)
  — a Legacy Contact uses an **access key + death certificate** to get iCloud data
  (the cleanest path by far).
- **After death without a Legacy Contact:** [Request access to a deceased family member's Apple Account](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102431).
- **Requires (U.S.):** a **court order** naming you as rightful inheritor that
  states: the decedent's name and Apple Account, that you (named) are next of
  kin/representative/heir, that the decedent was the user of all associated
  accounts, and that your authorization is "lawful consent" — plus a death
  certificate. Apple publishes the required court-order elements at the link above.
- **Purchases (iTunes/App Store):** licenses are generally **non-transferable**.

### Meta — Facebook
- **Memorialize or remove:** [Managing a deceased person's account](https://www.facebook.com/help/275013292838654)
  and the [special request / report form](https://www.facebook.com/help/1111566045566400).
  Immediate family can request **deletion** (proof of death required); anyone can
  request **memorialization**.
- **Plan-ahead tool:** [Legacy Contact](https://www.facebook.com/help/991335594313139)
  — can manage a memorialized profile and **download** shared content, but
  **cannot read private messages or log in**.
- **Content/messages:** not provided through the normal process; a court order is
  required and even then is limited.

### Meta — Instagram
- **Memorialize or remove:** [Report a deceased person's account](https://help.instagram.com/264154560391256).
  Removal requires proof you are an immediate family member (birth/death
  certificate, proof of authority).

### Microsoft (Outlook/Hotmail/MSN, OneDrive, Xbox)
- **Process:** the **Next of Kin / Custodian of Records** process — email
  **msrecord@microsoft.com** to start.
- **Requires:** proof you are legal next of kin + the decedent's death
  certificate + account details. Microsoft will either **ship a data DVD** of the
  contents or **close** the account, per your request (no self-service portal).

### Yahoo / AOL (Yahoo Mail, AOL Mail)
- **Policy:** accounts are **non-transferable**; there is **no right of
  survivorship**. You can request **closure**, billing suspension, and — in
  limited circumstances — content.
- **Requires for closure:** death certificate + proof of authority. For content,
  Yahoo/AOL require a **U.S. court order** and **will provide the required court-order
  language**.
- Links: [Yahoo](https://help.yahoo.com/kb/SLN2021.html) ·
  [AOL](https://help.aol.com/articles/options-available-if-an-account-owner-passes-away).

### X (formerly Twitter)
- **Process:** [Deactivate the account of a deceased person](https://help.x.com/en/forms/account-access/deactivate-or-close-account/deactivate-account-for-deceased).
  X works with a person authorized to act for the estate or a verified immediate
  family member to **deactivate** only.
- **Content/access:** X **does not provide account access or content** to anyone,
  regardless of relationship. Requires your ID + the decedent's death certificate.

### Amazon (account, Prime, Kindle, Photos, Audible)
- **Process:** no dedicated deceased-user portal — contact Amazon Customer Service
  to close the account; provide death certificate and proof of authority.
- **Digital content:** Prime Video, Amazon Music, Audible, and **Kindle** content
  is **licensed, not owned**, and generally **cannot be transferred or inherited**;
  access is lost when the account closes. Download/back up anything important
  before closing, and check Amazon Household / shared-library options.

## 4. A "court order to release contents" — what it must contain

Several providers (Google, Apple, Yahoo/AOL) require a **U.S. court order** before
releasing the *content* of communications. Drafting that order is attorney work
(unauthorized-practice concerns) — but the elements a Maine order generally needs
to track, drawn from **18-C §10-107** and the SCA, are:

- The **decedent's identity** and the specific **account(s)** (email address,
  Apple Account, handle).
- That the **decedent was the user** of all accounts associated with the request.
- That the **requestor** is the decedent's **personal representative / heir /
  authorized fiduciary**, with authority under Title 18-C.
- A finding that the requestor's authorization constitutes the **user's lawful
  consent** to disclosure under the Stored Communications Act (18 U.S.C. §2701
  et seq.).
- That disclosure is **not in violation** of the SCA or other applicable law.

**Use the provider's own template where one exists** — Apple publishes the
required order elements, and Yahoo/AOL will supply the required language on
request. Match the order to the provider's stated requirements; a generic order
is frequently rejected.

## 5. Practical workflow checklist

1. **Look for a planning tool first** — Google Inactive Account Manager, Apple
   Legacy Contact, Facebook Legacy Contact. If one exists, use it; it overrides
   other directions (§10-104) and avoids a court order.
2. **Order several certified death certificates** (Maine vital records, Title 22).
3. **Get your proof of authority** — letters from DE-101/DE-201/DE-301, or the
   AF-102 small-estate affidavit for a small estate.
4. **Decide what you actually need:** closure/billing-stop and non-content assets
   (catalogue, files, money) are easy; **message/email content** likely needs a
   **court order** (§10-107).
5. **Use each provider's official deceased-user form** (links above) — not regular
   support — and attach ID + death certificate + authority.
6. **Preserve before you close.** Download data while you still have access;
   closing an account can permanently delete content and licensed media.
7. **If content is refused,** ask the provider for its **required court-order
   language**, then have a Maine attorney seek an order tracking §10-107.
